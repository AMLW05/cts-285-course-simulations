DataMan Elicitation Case

You have a stakeholder concern. You do not yet have a requirement.

MLO 2.2 — Apply elicitation techniques to clarify ambiguous stakeholder statements and identify the need behind a request.
BriefingEvidenceManualConclusion

Incoming Stakeholder Concern

Ms. Alvarez — fourth-grade teacher, pilot classroom

“The kids get frustrated with it and stop trying. We need to fix that before I use it again.”

Ms. Alvarez is concerned. The project team wants an answer. Your job is to decide what should happen next.

What Do You Actually Know?

  • Ms. Alvarez has observed learners disengaging.
  • It mattered enough for her to raise it as a project concern.
  • You do not know what the learners were doing when it happened.
  • You do not know whether it follows a wrong answer, a score, or something else.
  • You do not know how often it happens, or to which learners.
  • You do not know what “frustrated” looked like from the outside.
Analyst rule: A concern is evidence that investigation is needed. It is not automatically a finished requirement.

Choose Your First Move

Each path reveals different evidence. Choose the action you think gives the project team the strongest next step.

There is not always one perfect elicitation technique. Your goal is enough trustworthy evidence to understand the problem before anyone commits to a solution.
DataMan Elicitation Case · Path A

Interview Ms. Alvarez

You chose to begin with the stakeholder who raised the concern.

You Ask…

“When you say the kids get frustrated, what were they doing right before you noticed it?”

Ms. Alvarez Responds

“It is usually after they miss one. They try again, miss again, and then they just put it down. A few of them start guessing anything to make it move on.”

You now know more — but the requirement is still incomplete.

Choose Your Follow-Up

“How often does this happen?”

Useful. Frequency helps establish scope and impact, although you still need to understand the practice session itself.

“What does the learner see after a wrong answer?”

Strong. This may reveal where feedback is missing from the current interaction.

“Should we add a hint button?”

Too early. A hint may eventually be appropriate, but you have not yet watched what a learner actually does.

“Which learners does it happen to most?”

Strong. This helps identify whose practice session should be observed next.

Evidence gained
  • Disengagement follows an incorrect answer, not the start of a session.
  • Learners make more than one attempt before giving up.
  • Some learners guess in order to move past the problem.
  • A learner's practice session is now important evidence.
DataMan Elicitation Case · Path B

You Jumped to a Solution

You recommended adaptive hints before defining the problem.

You Say…

“We could have the system detect when a learner is struggling and offer a hint automatically.”

Ms. Alvarez Responds

“Maybe. But I do not know that a hint is what they need. We do not even know what they are reacting to yet.”
Evidence

You selected a design solution before defining the stakeholder problem, the learner's actual experience, the trigger, or the success condition.

Requirements Before Solutions

Concern → Evidence → Requirement → Design Decision

Adaptive hints might eventually be evaluated as one possible design approach.

But right now, you still need to learn:

  • what the learner experiences after a wrong answer
  • when in the session it happens
  • how often it happens
  • what the original device was documented to do
  • what outcome the teacher actually needs
Recovery is part of analysis. Analysts will sometimes make an assumption or propose a solution too early. The important skill is recognizing the gap and returning to evidence gathering.
DataMan Elicitation Case · Path C

Observe a Practice Session

Instead of asking a learner to describe what happens, you ask to watch.

What You See

  1. The learner starts an Answer Checker session.
  2. A problem appears. The learner enters an answer.
  3. The answer is wrong. The device indicates it is wrong.
  4. The learner tries again. Wrong again.
  5. On the third miss, the device reveals the correct answer and moves on.
  6. The learner does not appear to expect this. They sit back.

The Learner Adds…

“I did not know it was going to do that. I thought I could keep trying. Now I do not know if I got it wrong or if I ran out.”

What Did Observation Reveal?

Specific trigger

Disengagement follows the third incorrect attempt, not the first.

Documented behavior

The three-attempt limit is a real, intended feature of the original device.

Learner understanding

The learner cannot tell how many attempts remain, so the reveal arrives without warning.

Potential need

The learner may need to know where they are in the attempt sequence.

Notice the shape of this finding. The device is doing what it was designed to do. The gap is between the documented behavior and what the learner understands about it. That is still a real requirement.

Is Observation Enough?

Reveal

Not necessarily.

Observation showed what happened in one session with one learner. The manual can tell you whether the three-attempt behavior is intentional and what it was for, which changes what you are allowed to recommend.

DataMan Elicitation Case · Document Analysis

Review the 1977 Manual

Existing documentation can confirm patterns that interviews and observation only suggest.

The original DataMan manual is project evidence. It tells you what the device was documented to do and, in places, why.

What the Manual Establishes

Documented behaviorWhat it tells the analyst
The Answer Checker indicates whether a submitted answer is correct.Immediate correctness feedback is original, intended behavior.
After repeated incorrect attempts, the device reveals the correct answer.The three-attempt limit is a designed feature, not a fault to be removed.
The device is described as reinforcing practice rather than testing.The stated purpose is to keep a learner practicing. Disengagement works against the product's own goal.
A score and time display is available to the learner.The device already shows the learner state about their own performance.

What Does the Evidence Support?

“The three-attempt limit is a defect and should be removed.”

Unsupported. The manual documents it as intended reinforcement behavior. Removing it is a design decision you have not yet earned the evidence to make.

“Learners are not told where they are in the attempt sequence.”

Supported. Observation showed a learner surprised by the reveal, and the manual documents the limit without documenting any warning before it. The gap is in what the learner is told.

“Adaptive hints are required to solve the problem.”

Unsupported. The evidence identifies a recurring problem but does not establish any particular implementation.

Evidence
  • The three-attempt behavior is documented and intentional.
  • The product's stated purpose is sustained practice.
  • Nothing in the documentation warns the learner before the reveal.
  • The evidence supports clarifying a feedback requirement, not removing a feature.
DataMan Elicitation Case · Conclusion

What Did You Surface?

The case began with a vague complaint. You now have enough evidence to describe a real requirement.

Your Evidence Trail

Stakeholder Concern → Interview → Observation → Document Analysis → Clarified Requirement

What Each Technique Added

TechniqueEvidence surfaced
InterviewMs. Alvarez clarified that disengagement follows repeated wrong answers, not the start of a session.
ObservationA learner was surprised when the device revealed the answer, and could not tell whether they had been wrong or had run out of attempts.
Document analysisThe manual establishes the three-attempt behavior as intentional, and documents no warning before the reveal.

Requirements Surfaced

Functional requirement

The system must show the learner how many attempts remain on the current problem before it reveals the correct answer.

Non-functional / interaction requirement

Feedback after an incorrect answer must be available through keyboard navigation and must not rely on color alone.

Notice What You Did Not Specify

  • a hint system
  • a countdown animation
  • a specific colour or icon
  • removal of the three-attempt limit
  • a specific programming library
You defined the requirement before designing the solution. You also left a documented feature intact, because the evidence did not support removing it.

Knowledge Check

Which statement best explains how elicitation produced the functional requirement?

Ms. Alvarez requested an attempt counter.

No. Ms. Alvarez reported only that learners were getting frustrated and giving up.

The manual specified that attempts must be displayed.

No. The manual documents the three-attempt behavior but documents no warning before the reveal. That absence is part of what the evidence surfaced.

Interview, observation, and document review gradually clarified the trigger, the learner's experience, and the documented behavior.

Exactly. The requirement emerged from three pieces of evidence rather than from one assumption or one solution request.

Mini Summary
  • Elicitation is evidence gathering, not order taking.
  • Different techniques reveal different kinds of information.
  • Documentation can protect an intended feature from being redesigned by assumption.
  • Analysts clarify the problem before selecting a solution.
  • The final requirement should be traceable back to stakeholder evidence.
