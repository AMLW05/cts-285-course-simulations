DataMan Design Investigation

Your backlog says what should move forward. Your job now is to decide what still needs to be understood before that work is safe and clear enough to build.

Decision boundary

Use your Module 3 product backlog, requirement traceability, acceptance criteria, and DataMan source evidence. Do not create documentation because a template expects it. Start with an implementation question, then choose the lightest evidence that can actually help answer it.

1. What kind of uncertainty are you investigating?

Choose the category that best describes the question you need answered for your selected DataMan product slice.

2. What evidence could answer that question?

Select one or two evidence types that would genuinely help. More artifacts are not automatically better.

3. Make the best current decision

4. New evidence changes the design environment

You do not have to change your decision just because new information appeared. You do have to evaluate whether the new evidence changes the question, the evidence you need, or the decision itself.

DataMan checks

Two attempts: Can the design distinguish try 1 from try 2 and support reveal after the second miss?

Memory Bank: Can the design represent or enforce the up-to-10-problem limit?

Results later: Can a curator retrieve a learner's past results without watching the session live?

XP values in use

Simplicity: Use the minimum evidence needed.

Feedback: Let evidence challenge the decision.

Courage: Revise weak decisions.

Communication: Make reasoning visible.

Respect: Different defensible designs may exist.
