DataMan Design Studio

Use representations as instruments. Your job is not to produce every diagram. Your job is to discover what the product work still needs you to know before it is safe enough to build.

Before you touch a representation

A design artifact earns its place only when it helps answer a question. A flow can expose missing states. An ERD can expose data that cannot be stored or retrieved. A wireframe can expose ambiguity, dead ends, and missing feedback. A small architecture sketch can expose responsibility in the wrong layer.

Studio rule: question first, evidence second, decision third.

Learn the tool

Behavior representations reveal time, state, and responsibility

A screen tells you what exists at one moment. A behavior flow tells you what happens between moments. For DataMan, that distinction matters because the first wrong attempt and the second wrong attempt do not produce the same consequence.

State

What must the system remember right now? Example: current problem, entered answer, try number.

Transition

What event changes the state? Example: Submit answer.

Responsibility

What decides what happens next? The interface can display feedback, but reusable business rules should not live only inside a screen.

Learn the tool

An ERD answers storage and relationship questions

Entities are things the system needs to remember independently. Attributes describe them. Relationships explain how records connect. Cardinality forces you to say whether one record can connect to one, many, or none of another.

Useful test: if you need to keep two occurrences apart, query them later, or attach their own facts to them, you may need a separate entity rather than another attribute.

Wireframes test what people can see, understand, and do

A wireframe is evidence about interaction—not decoration. It can expose a missing status, unclear next step, inaccessible feedback, or a dead end that an ERD cannot show.
